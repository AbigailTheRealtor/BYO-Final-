BidYourOffer — notice of changes to the Overture Places data
============================================================

APPLICATION-AUTHORED. NOTHING BELOW IS FOURSQUARE'S OR OVERTURE'S TEXT.

The upstream notice is a separate file and is reproduced verbatim there:

    resources/legal/foursquare-os-places-NOTICE.txt

That separation is deliberate. Foursquare's NOTICE permits appending a notice of
changes to it, but a reader skimming a single merged file cannot tell which
sentences Foursquare wrote and which we did — and the one thing the NOTICE
requires above all is that its content be preserved as theirs. So their file is
kept untouched and this one carries our statement.

This file exists to satisfy the second bullet of that NOTICE: "if applicable,
include prominent notices to the extent you've changed the Data." We have changed
the Data. This is what we changed.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. What we changed
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Pinned inputs (SSOT: config/overture_places.php):

    Overture data release      2026-06-17.0
    Overture schema version    v1.17.0
    Local corpus version       overture-2026-06-17.0-fl

(Storage details — which database holds the corpus and under what partition — are
deliberately omitted. This notice is published to the public and describes changes
to the DATA; how we store it is not a change to it, and naming our infrastructure
on a public page would tell a reader nothing about the licence and something about
our systems.)

(a) GEOGRAPHIC FILTERING — rows outside Florida were not imported.
    Bounding box, EPSG:4326 degrees (config/overture_places.php regions.florida):
    west -87.63, south 24.40, east -79.97, north 31.00.
    The box is coarse and admits some water and slivers of Georgia and Alabama;
    it errs toward keeping a row rather than dropping a real coastal address.

(b) CONFIDENCE FILTERING — rows below an existence-confidence floor of 0.90
    were not imported (config/overture_places.php confidence_min). Overture's
    `confidence` is numeric in [0,1].

(c) CATEGORY FILTERING AND CROSSWALK — only rows whose Overture
    `categories.primary` token appears in an eight-entry map were imported, and
    each was crosswalked to one of our own canonical category keys
    (App\Services\Spatial\OvertureCategoryMap). Seven of those categories are
    servable to users (App\Services\LocationDna\Providers\CorpusPoiCategoryMap).
    Overture's v1.17.0 `basic_category` and `taxonomy` vocabularies are NOT
    mapped; an unmapped token is tallied and left unmapped rather than inferred.

(d) SCHEMA NORMALIZATION — records were mapped from the Overture schema into this
    application's own spatial schema
    (App\Services\Spatial\OverturePlaceNormalizer): field renaming, geometry
    reduced to a point, and our own corpus/version columns added.

(e) PER-SOURCE ATTRIBUTION WAS NOT RETAINED, AND THIS IS THE CHANGE THAT MATTERS
    MOST FOR ATTRIBUTION. The Overture record carries `sources[].dataset`, naming
    which member supplied the row. Our normalizer COUNTS those datasets into an
    integer `source_count` and discards the names; the stored row carries
    `source = 'overture'` and an `attrs` holding only `geometry_type`.

    The consequence: we cannot tell which of our rows came from Foursquare, and
    therefore cannot tell which are Apache-2.0 rather than CDLA-Permissive-2.0 or
    CC0-1.0. We do not guess. Every row is treated as though it could be the
    Foursquare slice, so the Apache-2.0 obligations — the copy of the license, this
    notice of changes, and preservation of the upstream NOTICE — are applied to
    the whole corpus.

    Recovering per-row precision is a corpus change (retaining `sources[].dataset`
    and re-importing), not a configuration change, and is deliberately not faked
    in the meantime.

(f) MEASURED SIZE — the Florida extract measured 29,434 rows.

NOT CHANGED: the substance of individual place records. We filter, crosswalk and
restructure; we do not edit place names, coordinates or attributes, and we add no
places of our own to the corpus.
